Hear your phone through your Mac's headphones.

Plays your Android phone's audio out of whatever your Mac is already playing to. Handy when your headphones only hold one connection at a time, as AirPods Max do.

How it reacts

You never start this, stop it, or switch it over. Pick a situation and see what the app does about it on its own.

Streaming

Audio starts by itself

The app checks your output device twice a second, so it notices yours appear and begins within about a second. You do not click anything, and the window does not need to be open.

Where the 191 milliseconds go

Every figure here was measured on real hardware. Most of the delay is not ours to fix, and it depends on what you listen through.

Capture buffer15 ms
Output buffer5 ms
Bluetooth, fixed170.7 ms

That last row happens inside the headphones, not on your Mac. It is 89% of the wired budget and no setting on either machine changes it, so everything this software controls is the other twenty milliseconds. The figure was measured with AirPods Max; other Bluetooth headphones differ, and a wired output device or a USB DAC skips that stage almost entirely. Over Wi-Fi the capture buffer grows to 200 ms, which is fine for music and noticeable on video.

Quick start

Three commands. You build it yourself, so there is nothing to notarize and no Gatekeeper prompt to work around.

# adb comes from Android Studio, or install platform-tools separately brew install scrcpy git clone https://github.com/nicglazkov/pixel-audio-bridge.git cd pixel-audio-bridge && ./build.sh

Then plug the phone in, accept the debugging prompt, and open build/PixelAudioBridge.app. For wireless, run ./bin/pab enable-wireless once while it is plugged in.
